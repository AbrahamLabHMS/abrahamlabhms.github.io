### Publication checker maintenance

- The existing workflow now runs weekly on Monday at 14:17 UTC, plus manual dispatch. This is repository configuration only until main commits/pushes it; no schedule or issue was activated during this local task.
- Local DOI/PMID identity, title-based possible work identity, and preprint/journal version identity are separate. Same-title new DOIs remain review candidates. Newly indexed known DOIs are identifier updates. Pinned preprint citations are compared to the cited version while newest-version notices and journal relations are tracked separately.
- PubMed and Crossref compare title, ordered authors, journal/provider, identifiers where supplied, and preprint/journal status. bioRxiv supplies version-specific metadata and publication relations; Crossref journal targets are checked independently. A PMID or a `Journal Article` tag alongside `Preprint` does not establish peer review.
- Reviewed source differences remain visible in `acceptedMetadataVariations`, with citation evidence, instead of generating repeat false alarms. Exact full-value matching prevents the exceptions from hiding future name changes, additions, removals, order changes, different sources, or a newer preprint version. Title, journal, and status checks remain independent.
- Crossref online, issue, and posted dates remain separate fields in `report.json`. Missing issue metadata is not invented. Automated runs do not advance the public review date.
- `output/publication-check/report.md` and `report.json` are standalone read-only artifacts, including partial results on source failure. Metadata discrepancies produce a nonzero checker result; new candidates and versions alone require review but do not mean the source check failed.
- A default-branch run maintains one marker-identified, bot-owned review issue. Workflow concurrency serializes runs; all open and closed issues are paginated before mutation. Unchanged finding fingerprints produce no writes or comments. Changed findings update the same managed section, preserving maintainer notes; new findings can reopen the same closed issue. No automatic closure or publication occurs. Duplicate/ambiguous issues, oversized bodies, inventory failures, and uncertain writes stop mutation and leave the artifact for review.
- Human approval is required for citation edits, journal transitions, corresponding-author eligibility, and summaries. A published successor should replace, not duplicate, the public entry only after primary-source verification.
